# The prospect gate

**What this is:** the single owner of the qualification rules for the outbound motion. Who is worth
a connection request, who is worth a personalized opener, and who is worth nothing but a scroll.

**Who applies it:** `skills/prospect-brief`, on every run. The skill walks this ladder as written.
It never invents a kill, never adds a scoring weight, and never restates the rulebook inside a
brief. It names which row fired and shows the evidence, which is what makes a verdict auditable.
If the skill and this file ever disagree, this file wins and the skill gets corrected in the same
session.

**Stale after:** 2027-09-01 (a year past the template's release so a fresh clone stays quiet; re-verify the tells against the platform before relying on them). Half the tells below are read off profile page furniture and public
site copy, and both drift. Re-derive the tells against a real batch before trusting this past that
date.

**What this file does not own:** the offer, its price, the qualification bar for the offer itself,
and whether a copy freeze is currently on. All four live in
`knowledge/business/outbound-offer.md`. The message wording lives in
`templates/message-templates.md`. No price appears anywhere in this file, on purpose.

---

## The three rules that outrank every test below

**1. Only hard kills kill. Tells score.** A single tell is a note, never a verdict. A tell section
fails only on two or more tells, and two failing sections kill. This exists because a ladder built
as a stack of independent any-one-fires conditions kills almost everyone: at a ten percent false
positive rate per condition, twenty conditions leave a real buyer roughly a twelve percent chance
of surviving. That arithmetic is what a zero-send day looks like from the inside.

**2. Nobody is disqualified on revenue.** Not on revenue, not on headcount, not on client volume.
No public profile shows any of those numbers, so a revenue-based kill is a guess about a stranger
dressed up as a filter. They are call qualifiers, and the call is where they get asked. If a brief
finds itself estimating revenue, it stops and writes "unknown". This is a hard rule and it has no
exception, including the one where the estimate feels obvious.

**3. The queue never closes at zero.** If a block of 20 reviewed profiles yields fewer than 5 at
SEND or FILL, send the 5 highest-ranked hard-kill survivors that day anyway, ranked by fewest
tells, then log the yield and rebuild the search. Holding sends to build a better list tomorrow
feels like diligence and is the most reliable way to run a motion that never actually starts. The
ceiling on how many requests a day is safe to send is not this file's to set: that is
`knowledge/business/linkedin-safety-rules.md`.

## The corridor

**In the corridor:** the person who can say yes, at a business that visibly runs a manual process
between two systems that do not talk.

- Owner, founder, managing partner, COO, GM, head of operations, practice manager, ops lead.
- A business with staff. Enough people that a manual process has a named owner and a real cost.
- An operating business with transactions to track: retail, distribution, field service, clinics
  and practices, logistics, light manufacturing, trades, professional services with client intake.
- More than one system of record in daily use. That is the whole thesis of the offer.

**Outside the corridor:**

- Anyone who cannot spend without a committee or a procurement cycle.
- Anyone selling what you sell: automation shops, integration consultancies, no-code studios,
  fractional CTO practices, "we build internal tools" agencies.
- A software vendor whose own product is the connector. You would be selling plumbing to a plumber.
- A business large enough to run its own platform or data team. The pain has an owner already.
- Pre-revenue, or a solo operator with one system and nothing to bridge. Nothing to automate yet,
  only something to invent.

**Deliberately not criteria:** revenue, funding, headcount past the floor tell below, the tools
they already pay for, how polished their website is, or whether they seem like the sort of person
who buys things.

## Layer 0 - fix the pool before you open a profile

Cheaper than every test below, and the highest-leverage thing in the motion.

- Search by **operating vertical plus an ops-shaped title** before broad keyword nets. A broad net
  returns practitioners, employees and people holding three portfolio roles at once, and you spend
  the whole session killing them one at a time.
- **Measure yield on the last 10 reviewed, never cumulatively.** Search results are ordered by
  relevance, so the real owners are front-loaded and the tail fills with noise. A rolling window
  sees the page turn. A running total averages a good first page against a dead fourth one and
  tells you to keep going.
- **Under roughly 30 percent SEND plus FILL on that rolling window, stop and build the next
  search.** The pool is the problem, not the prospect in front of you.

## Layer 0.5 - reachability, before any reading

**Try the connect first. Read the profile only if it goes through.** Some accounts restrict
invitations to people who already have their email address, and the request is refused. Nothing on
the page announces this in advance, so a careful read can be spent on someone who was never
reachable at all. An unreachable profile is not a lead, whatever it would have scored.

Post-accept, this layer is moot and the brief records it as such. The connection already landed.

## Layer 1 - hard kills

Each row is mechanical or resolved by one named test. Any hit kills. Name the row that fired.

| # | Kill on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not the buyer.** An individual-contributor or middle-manager title at someone else's company, or an open-to-work banner | Cannot say yes. The highest-frequency kill, and the most expensive one to get wrong in either direction |
| 2 | **Peer vendor.** Sells automation, integrations, internal tools, RPA, no-code builds, custom software, or "we digitize your operations" | Selling into the same room. They are competition or they are a partner conversation, not a prospect |
| 3 | **Single-system shape**, decided by the systems test below | If nothing is re-typed, exported or reconciled, the pain does not exist there |
| 4 | **Procurement shape.** Public sector, a hospital or university system, or anything that runs vendor onboarding, security review and a tender | A solo operator cannot serve that cycle and should not spend a quarter learning that |
| 5 | **Franchise or shared-services shape.** "Franchise Owner", "licensee", `<Brand> of <City>`, or an operating unit inside a group with a central back office | The systems and the process belong to the franchisor or the group. The person in front of you cannot change either |
| 6 | **Exit or wind-down.** "acquired by", "now part of", "formerly", "exited", an end-dated current role, or a current role at the acquiring company | Nobody installs a new system into a business they are leaving |
| 7 | **Already ours.** A current client, an open proposal, or a KILL logged here inside the last 90 days | Route to the client file or the prior brief. A second cold approach to a live client is the worst message you will send this year |

### The systems test, which owns kill 3

"All-in-one", "we run everything in one place" and a single named platform in the copy are **flags,
not kills**. Every business claims to be tidy. The token has to describe how work actually moves,
not how they market themselves.

When the flag fires, ask one question out loud and record the answer with its evidence:
**what does one new order, job, patient, shipment or client actually touch, start to finish?**

- **Starts and ends in one system.** No export, no re-typing, no spreadsheet in the middle, no
  person carrying a number from one screen to another. **KILL.** There is nothing to bridge.
- **Touches a second system, a spreadsheet, a portal, a PDF, or a human who re-types it.** **PASS**,
  even if the copy insists everything is integrated. Especially then.

A kill reached by reading the row's intent is auditable. One reached by pattern-matching the row's
title is not.

## Layer 2 - tells, scored by section

A section fails on **two or more** tells. One tell is a note. Record the tell count per section next
to the verdict so the ratio stays auditable and this file can be corrected against real outcomes.

**FLOOR - too small for the pain to cost anything**

- No named staff anywhere: no team page, no "our team", no second person in any copy.
- First-person singular where a business writes we. "I help", "I work with".
- A personal-name domain as the entire business presence.
- The business is under a year old with no visible customers.
- One system, one person, and everything lives in the inbox.

**CEILING - somebody already owns this**

- A named internal IT, data, engineering or platform function.
- Open roles for an integration engineer, data engineer, systems analyst or RevOps lead.
- An engineering blog, a developer portal, or a public API of their own.
- Program-management language: steering committee, vendor management, a change board.

**ALREADY-SOLVED - the process has an owner or a finished answer**

- Copy describing their integration program as done, with the platform named and the result stated.
- A case study of their own automation work.
- A current employee whose title is literally Automation, Systems, or Business Applications.
- **The builder.** The owner's answer to ops pain is to build it themselves, and they post about
  it: an internal script, a homemade dashboard, "I wired our two systems together over a weekend".
  Deliberately a **tell and not a kill**. Someone running a janky homemade system has already
  priced the pain, already lost a weekend to it, and is often the easiest sale in the batch once it
  breaks. It fails this section only alongside a second tell.

**VELOCITY - right size, wrong rate of change**

- Nothing has moved in years: same locations, same stack, same copy, same team.
- Explicit anti-software posture, "we do it the old way and it works".
- Buying language that implies a committee without meeting the procurement kill.
- A named change freeze, or peak season with the whole business heads-down.

**DORMANCY - read the date, not the count**

Most recent activity older than roughly 90 days, or no activity at all on a thin profile, is a hard
demote to FILL at best. A dormant account cannot accept a request and cannot reply to a message.

Two ways this gets misread. **Read activity, not authored posts:** authored articles are often years
old on an account that likes and comments daily, and scoring off them turns "active yesterday" into
"six years dead". And **absence of authored posts is not a floor tell.** Most real operators never
post anything.

**Flags, never kills, never tells on their own**

- **Location.** A profile pin matches a registered address, and registered-agent mail suites are
  cheap. The country subdomain in a profile URL reflects the data provider's exit node, not the
  prospect. Read operating signals instead: where their named clients are, what currency their
  pricing is in, where the team sits.
- **Timezone overlap.** A schedule with no overlap at all is a delivery constraint worth noting in
  the brief. It is not a reason to skip a qualified buyer.
- **Tooling.** A prospect already paying for a tool in the category has already priced the pain.
  That is closer to a buying signal than a disqualifier.

## Layer 3 - the ladder

Ordered. First line that fits wins. No double-matching, no averaging.

1. Any **Layer 1** hit -> **KILL**
2. **Two or more** Layer 2 sections failing -> **KILL**
3. Ops-authority title, **plus** a named second system or an observable manual process, **plus** a
   change signal (hiring an ops or admin role, a new location, a migration or system-switch post)
   -> **SEND**
4. Ops-authority title present, process evidence **absent rather than contradicted** -> **FILL**
5. Exactly one Layer 2 section failing, everything else clean -> **FILL**
6. Everything else -> **KILL**

**Multi-owner note for line 3.** When two or more owners are named, read which function this one
holds. Operations, delivery, finance or COO is SEND-eligible. Sales or brand with a partner on
operations is FILL, and the brief notes the operations partner's name for the call.

**The dormancy cap applies after the ladder.** A dormant account that lands on line 3 comes out at
FILL, not SEND. It is a cap on the verdict, not a section failure, so it never contributes to the
two-sections count in line 2. Nobody spends an opener on an account that cannot accept.

## The three verdict vocabularies

The gate always emits **SEND**, **FILL** or **KILL**. The moment you are in decides what that
verdict is called, because the three moments spend different things.

| Gate says | Pre-send screening | Post-accept triage | Inbound triage |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEND | SEND the request | `ICP`, write the opener | `corridor` |
| FILL | FILL, send it, do not read further | `SKIP` the opener | `unknown` |
| KILL | KILL | `SKIP` | `DQ` |

**FILL is the row that needs the rule.** FILL is a connect-stage answer meaning "worth a request,
not worth a read". By post-accept the connection has already landed, so the only thing FILL can
still decide is whether this person earns the expensive act, which is the personalized opener. They
do not. Opener slots are capacity-bound and they belong to the clear yeses. Say that plainly in the
brief instead of promoting FILL to ICP because the person is already connected.

**`SKIP` is not a verdict on the human.** Neither is KILL. Both are a read of a public profile
against one offer at one moment, with half the evidence missing. Borderline calls are the
operator's, always.

## The copy freeze

**What a freeze is:** a period during which the opener, the follow-up and the delivery message are
read **verbatim** from `templates/message-templates.md` and only the bracket slots are filled. No
rewrites. No "this one needs a different angle". No improvement.

**Why:** a reply rate is only a measurement if it measures one message. Thirty conversations
against thirty slightly different openers measures nothing, and the instinct to improve each one is
exactly what destroys the sample. A freeze buys a real answer at the cost of some elegance.

**While a freeze holds:**

- A brief that seems to need a new line of copy does not need one. Personalization lives in the
  specific detail cited in the delivery notes, never in new sentences.
- `skills/prospect-brief` will not compose opener, follow-up or delivery copy at all.
- An objection with no send-safe response in an owner file is recorded as unanswered. Nobody
  invents handling for it mid-batch.

**This file does not declare the freeze.** Whether one is currently on, and the exact release
condition, live in `knowledge/business/outbound-offer.md`. This file owns what a freeze means and
what it forbids. That file owns whether one is running.

## What this gate does not do

- **It does not screen revenue, headcount or client volume.** Rule 2, above. Those are call
  qualifiers and they get asked on the call.
- **It does not kill on tooling.** Buying a tool in the category, or building a janky one, both
  mean the pain has already been priced.
- **It does not judge the human.** See the note under the vocabulary table.
- **It does not decide whether to send.** The operator sends, on that specific message, per the
  `CLAUDE.md` pre-send checklist, item 7.
- **It does not claim a time cost per profile.** The honest answer is that nobody has timed a block
  yet. A hard kill is fast and a survivor is not, and a made-up average would set a daily target
  that quietly fails every day. No number goes on this line until one block of 10 has actually been
  timed with a clock.
