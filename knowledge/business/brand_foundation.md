# Brand foundation

Internal working doctrine for Northwind Labs. Positioning, who we are for, what we solve, what we
can honestly claim, and what we refuse to do.

**This is not marketing copy.** Nothing here is written to be read by a buyer. It is written so
that every post, every outbound message and every proposal comes out of one head instead of five.
Buyer-facing wording is derived from this, never lifted from it.

**This file carries no prices.** It used to. The productized offer's price now lives in exactly
one place, `knowledge/business/outbound-offer.md`, and engagement prices live in the client's own
`knowledge/clients/<client>/roadmap.md`. That change was made because a re-price written in two
files is a dead number sitting in one of them, and this file is the one every piece of outbound
flows from, so it is the worst possible place to keep a stale figure.

Living document. It changes when the work teaches something, not on a schedule.

## 1. The four questions

The frame is: what outcome, known for what, doing what, learning what. Answer them in that order,
then stop re-answering them every week.

### Q1 - Outcome

Recurring revenue with a client base that cannot be taken out by one email.

Three conditions, all of them, not one of them:

- at least three paying clients
- no single client above about 40 percent of collected revenue
- at least one recurring line, so a slow month is not a zero month

Today the business fails all three. Two clients on the books, one of them the whole of collected
revenue, all of it project-based, no recurring line. That is the shape that has to change, not the
size. The measured figure is generated, never typed here:
`knowledge/business/MONEY.md`, `Concentration by owner` section, regenerated by `scripts/money`
on every run. The revenue and buffer targets live in that same generated file.

**One note on where the target lives.** This file owns the three conditions above as doctrine.
`scripts/money` renders the same ceiling next to the measured position so the target and the
reality are read together, which means the constant in that script and this paragraph move in the
same session or they disagree silently. Nothing else in the repo may restate it.

The composition problem is the reason the productized retainer exists at all. See
`knowledge/business/outbound-offer.md`.

### Q2 - Known for

> The person a small business calls when two systems that should talk to each other do not, and
> somebody has been covering the gap by hand for a year.

Not "AI consultant". Not "automation expert". Both are crowded categories where the buyer has
already been burned by someone selling a workflow diagram. The specific, checkable claim is
narrower and it is the one we can actually prove.

**Internal wording versus buyer wording.** Internally: data plumbing and reliability. To a buyer:
"the weekly stock count stops being a person's Tuesday." Translate to their process, never to
their technology.

### Q3 - Do

**One buyer, two shapes of work.**

Buyer: an operations-heavy small or mid-size business, roughly 10 to 100 people, running two or
more systems of record that do not integrate, where a named person absorbs the gap manually.

- **Client builds.** Phased, fixed price, one phase quoted at a time. Discovery first, always
  paid, always small. Then build. Then whatever the discovery proved was worth building. Each
  phase is quoted on its own and the roadmap file is the record.
- **Ops Automation Sprint.** The productized version of the same thing, sized to two weeks and
  one problem, for buyers who do not need a phased engagement. Scope, price, gate and
  qualification bar: `knowledge/business/outbound-offer.md`.

The sprint is not a lead magnet for the builds. It is a real deliverable that stands alone. If it
turns into a bigger engagement, that engagement gets its own roadmap file and its own prices.

### Q4 - Learn

Learning happens through delivery, not before it. Two open questions, both answered by doing:

1. **Does the sprint sell without a phased engagement behind it?** Answered by the gate in
   `outbound-offer.md`, at 30 logged conversations. Not before.
2. **Which pain sells fastest?** Answered by the pain-point bank's promotion column, which only
   moves on evidence from real conversations. `knowledge/pain-points/pain-point-bank.md`.

Neither question gets a theory. Both get a count.

## 2. The problem, stated the way it actually shows up

A business buys a system for one job and a second system for another job. Both are fine. Neither
talks to the other. So a person becomes the integration: exporting, re-typing, reconciling,
chasing the difference.

Nobody ever decided to do this. It accumulated. That is why it survives budget reviews: there is
no line item called "somebody re-types invoices every Thursday". The cost is real and invisible at
the same time.

What makes it a buyable problem rather than a grumble:

- **It has a name attached.** A specific person loses a specific block of time on a schedule.
- **It has already survived one internal fix attempt.** Usually a spreadsheet, sometimes a script
  someone's nephew wrote. That failure is what makes them willing to pay this time.
- **It gets worse with growth.** Every new location, product line or staff member multiplies it.
  Which means the pain has a clock on it and they can feel it ticking.

The catalogue of the specific shapes this takes is `knowledge/pain-points/pain-point-bank.md`.
The market scan of who else sells into it is
`knowledge/research/smb-ops-tooling-scan-2026-07-24.md`.

## 3. The line we keep repeating

> An automation is not finished until it can tell you it broke.

Four ideas in one sentence: monitoring, alerting, honesty about failure, and the fact that most
of what gets sold as automation has none of them. It is also the difference the buyer can verify
in week one, which is why it survives contact with a skeptic.

Usage: say it explicitly in early content, with the mechanism attached. After enough repetition
it becomes shorthand for everything already said and can carry weight on its own.

## 4. Contrarian column

Left is what the category says. Right is our counter-position. Right-hand entries are the content
backlog. Add an entry when a real conversation produces one, not when a thought is available.

**1.** LEFT: "Automate everything with AI."
RIGHT: Most of the win is deterministic plumbing. AI belongs at the one step that genuinely needs
judgment, and nowhere else. Adding a probabilistic component to a job that has a right answer is
how you get an automation nobody trusts.

**2.** LEFT: "We built it in a no-code tool, so it is a system."
RIGHT: A flow with no error handling, no retries and no alerting is a demo. It works until the
first malformed record, then it fails quietly and someone finds out at month end.

**3.** LEFT: "Buy the integration platform, it connects everything."
RIGHT: Platforms connect products. Your problem is the exceptions: the returns, the split
shipments, the one location that does it differently. The exceptions are the job.

**4.** LEFT: "Rip out the old system, it is the root cause."
RIGHT: The old system is where the money is recorded and where everyone knows how to work. A
migration is an eighteen-month project that solves the problem by replacing it with a bigger one.
Sync it instead.

**5.** LEFT: "You need a data warehouse first."
RIGHT: You need one job that runs nightly and shouts when it fails. Build the warehouse when
somebody has a question the nightly job cannot answer.

**6.** LEFT: "AI can just do the work."
RIGHT: Anything the automation does on your behalf is your liability, not the vendor's. So it
gets exactly the permissions it needs to do its job and not one more.

**7.** LEFT: "It saves 20 hours a week."
RIGHT: Nobody's week actually shrinks by 20 hours. What changes is that the reconciliation stops
being a person's problem and starts being a thing that either ran or did not. Sell the removed
liability, not a fantasy timesheet.

## 5. Proof, and only what we can actually claim

Delivered, checkable, and referenceable with permission:

- **A multi-location retailer.** Two phases delivered and signed off: a discovery that mapped both
  systems and named the failure points, then a nightly two-way inventory sync with a
  reconciliation report and alerting. A third phase is proposed. State, scope and prices:
  `knowledge/clients/acme-retail/roadmap.md`.
- **A small clinic group.** A paid scoping diagnostic on a re-typing problem across two systems.
  Delivered; no build scoped yet. State: `knowledge/clients/beacon-health/README.md`.

What we **cannot** claim yet, and must not imply:

- No case study with a client-verified number attached. "Saved them X hours" is not on the record
  and does not go in copy until it is.
- No sprint has been sold. The productized offer has zero closes. Any content that implies
  otherwise is a lie with a short shelf life.
- No named-logo permission. Ask before a client's name goes anywhere public.

`.claude/agents/fact-check.md` exists to catch exactly this class of drift before it ships.

## 6. What Northwind Labs does not do

Written down because every one of these is a thing that will be asked for, and the answer is
faster if it is already decided.

- **No staff augmentation.** Not an extra pair of hands on somebody's backlog. Fixed scope,
  fixed price, defined end.
- **No system replacements or migrations.** We sync what exists. See column entry 4.
- **No ongoing custom development retainers.** The care retainer covers what we built. It is not
  a monthly dev budget.
- **No reselling or white-labelling through an agency.** One layer between us and the person with
  the problem is one layer too many for a two-week sprint.
- **No AI-led framing.** The word does not lead a proposal, a page or an outbound message. It
  describes a component, when a component is genuinely what it is.
- **No unverified timelines.** A date is given after Sam has personally checked it, never from
  optimism in a live conversation. Ruled 2026-07-19, `ai/DECISIONS.md`, after exactly that
  mistake produced a missed date and an apology.
- **No free pilots.** The paid diagnostic is the smallest thing we sell and it stays paid.
- **No advice outside the builder's seat.** Systems, automation and reliability are defensible.
  How to run someone's clinic is not.

## 7. Operating discipline

**Input metrics.** These are the only ones tracked, weekly, in
`knowledge/ops/logbook/STATS.md`:

- conversations started with a qualified person
- posts shipped that carry a mechanism, not just a diagnosis
- proposals sent

Deliberately not tracked: impressions, likes, followers, reply rate on a single message. All of
them move for reasons unrelated to whether the business works.

**The comfort trap.** The documented failure mode is retreating into building when selling is the
uncomfortable thing. Every internal project answers one question before it starts: is this revenue
work, or is this avoidance wearing a keyboard? If avoidance, close the editor and send messages.

**Empty beats filler.** The content metric is "did something on-thesis ship", not "did something
ship". But delivery generates on-thesis material as exhaust, so having nothing to say is a signal
that the thesis is blunt, not a licence to post filler.

**Voice.** Owned by `knowledge/voice/copy-rules.md` and `knowledge/voice/channel-voice.md`. Not
restated here. The one rule worth naming twice because it leaks: never justify the work, the
price, or the decision unasked.
